# Writing style
How the learner writes, from their blog posts (`~/Documents/PhD/blog/blog/posts`: CNN motif finder, Markov chain, hifiasm, experimental design, how I use Claude). Write sheet text, primers and asides like this.

## Voice
- First person, talking to a friend who also does science. "I", "we", "let's", "you".
- Casual words are fine: "okay", "so", "pretty much", "a bit", "super simple", "gets old fast", "wanna" (sparingly).
- Starts sentences with "So,", "Okay,", "And", "But". Uses "Now let's ..." to move on.
- Small honest asides in parentheses. "(I've left out trio binning, which deserves its own post)". "(is this matrix symmetric?)".
- Mild self-deprecating or salty humor, never a punchline. "Okay, but this is dumb. Nucleotides don't appear at random."
- Admits limits plainly. "This is an optimistic bound." "for now".

## Shape
- Motivation first: why I care, in one or two sentences. Then the idea. Then the math.
- Build up: start with the dumb simple version, say why it's not enough, then add the next piece.
- Concrete before abstract. A tiny example, then the general form.
- Italics for a term the first time it shows up: *transition matrix*, *score*.
- Throws a question at the reader mid-text instead of a callout box.
- Mixes short sentences with longer, comma-joined ones. Not every sentence is crisp.

## Avoid
- Slogans and grand claims. Not "This is the heart of attention." More like "Honestly, most of attention is this, plus some bookkeeping."
- Tidy triplets and parallel lines that sound like a poster ("Same direction: large. Right angle: zero.").
- "Key insight", "crucially", "powerful", "elegant", "Let's dive in", "journey", "unlock".
- Imperatives stacked like a form ("Write its letter.", "Write yes or no."). Ask like a person would.
- Em dashes and emojis (global rule).

## Examples
- Robotic: "This one multiply-and-add is the heart of attention. The rest of the month is built around it."
- Better: "Honestly, most of attention is this one number plus some bookkeeping. We'll keep coming back to it."
- Robotic: "Before computing anything: which key points in exactly the same direction as q?"
- Better: "Just by looking, which key points the exact same way as q?"
