## Part 3 · Why 5′→3′, from first principles (4 min)

Every DNA polymerase extends 5′→3′; none extends 3′→5′. A hypothetical 3′→5′ enzyme would
hold a growing chain with a **5′-triphosphate end** and add nucleotides onto that end.

Polymerases proofread: they excise a misincorporated nucleotide, then re-extend.

**3.1** After the excision step, what is left behind at the growing end of the
hypothetical 3′→5′ chain? One line.

**3.2** Why is that catastrophic for the hypothetical enzyme but harmless for the real
5′→3′ one? Two sentences maximum.

*This is the standard argument, not a trick. Once you have it you never memorize the
direction again — you re-derive it.*
