## Part 3 · Perturbation (7 min)

**3.1** You isolate a mutant polymerase with a **damaged thumb domain**. Palm and fingers are
intact; the catalytic site is normal; it still adds correct nucleotides at a normal rate.

Predict the **error spectrum**. Specifically: does the rate of **substitutions** or of
**frameshifts** rise, and **where in the genome** would you look to detect it most easily?
Two sentences. *(Everything you need is in your day-02 Part 4 answer plus the reading.)*

**3.2** DNA polymerases fall into five families with essentially no sequence homology between
them. Yet the **palm** is structurally well conserved across all five, while the **fingers**
and **thumb** are built from different sequences that happen to fold into analogous shapes.

**Why would evolution constrain the palm much more tightly than the other two?** One
sentence. *(What is the palm holding, and how much freedom does that leave?)*

## Part 4 · Stretch (only if time remains — skip freely, no penalty)

The proofreading exonuclease has its **own catalytic site, ~30 Å away** from the polymerase
site. To edit, the enzyme must melt the last few base pairs of the primer terminus, swing the
3′ end all the way across to the other site, cut, and swing back. It is slow and elaborate.

An engineer would put both chemistries in one site and save the trip.

**Give one reason the cell is better off with the sites far apart.** Two sentences. *(Think
about what a single site would have to be shaped like to do both jobs, and about how often
you want the editing decision to fire.)*
